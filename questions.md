# Client Questionnaire

Short answers are fine. Bullet points are better than paragraphs. "I don't know yet" is a real answer.

## A. The business

A1 What does your company do? (One or two sentences is plenty.)
> Sets the vocabulary. What the system calls things comes from how you describe the work.

A2 Which retailers do you sell into, and how many brands do you represent? (Walmart, Sam's, Target, Kroger, Dollar General, HEB, Costco, UNFI, KeHE, McLane… a rough count is fine.)
> Tells us how big the account and brand lists need to be, and whether one deal can span several retailers.

A3 Attach your logo and the logos of the brands you represent. (Vector files if you have them, otherwise the biggest PNG you can find. A link to where they live works too, and "send later" is a fine answer.)
> So it looks like your company the first time you open it, instead of something you have to rebrand later.

## B. Who's in it

B1 Who would log in? (Names and roles. Mark whoever should be the admin.)
> We build the accounts before you ever see it, so your first login is your real one.

B2 Should everyone see everything, or should some people only see their own accounts? (If it's the second, say who's limited to what.)
> Permissions are cheap to set now and disruptive to change once people have been working in it.

## C. How the work actually runs

C1 Attach the spreadsheets and documents you run the business on today. (Worth more than the rest of this form put together. If you'd rather not send files, list what they are and what's in them.)
> Your spreadsheets are the real spec. They show the fields you actually use, which beats any description of them.

C2 List the stages a deal goes through, first conversation to product on the shelf. (Your names for them, not ours. A list is fine — no sentences needed.)
> These become the actual pipeline. If we guess them, every deal gets logged in the wrong place.

C3 When are your line reviews, and how far ahead do you start working them? (Name the resets or review windows that set your calendar.)
> Your calendar drives every reminder. We can't time one without knowing what it's counting down to.

C4 What falls through the cracks most often? (One example is enough — ideally the last one that cost you.)
> This is what the system is for. Name the failure and we'll build the thing that catches it.

## D. Ground rules

D1 What software do you use today, and which of it can't change? (Retail Link, Luminate, NIQ or Circana, Excel, QuickBooks, Google Workspace or Outlook, Dropbox, DocuSign, EDI providers.)
> Separates what we replace from what we work around. Anything you can't give up, we build to sit beside.

D2 Have you used a CRM before? (Which one, and what made you stop.)
> What made you quit the last one is the most useful warning you can give us.

## W. If you also want a website (optional)

Skip this whole section if a website isn't part of this — nothing here affects the CRM.

W1 Do you already own a domain? (Who has the login, and is anything live there now?)
> Decides whether we launch on something you already own or buy and set one up for you.

W2 What should a visitor do on the site? (Pick one: call you, fill out a form, download a line sheet, book a meeting.)
> One goal per site. Naming it up front is the difference between a site that works and a brochure.

W3 Should website leads land in the CRM automatically? (And who gets the notification?)
> Tells us whether to build the bridge between the two, and whose phone it lights up.
